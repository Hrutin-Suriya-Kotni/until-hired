# Writing in the candidate's voice

Recruiters read hundreds of applications. The ones that sound like a real person, specific and plain, get read to the end.

## Do
- Write the way the candidate talks (learn it from their writing samples and intake answers).
- Lead with the specific: a product name, a number, a decision they made.
- Use short words and active verbs: built, cut, shipped, fixed, led, measured.
- Vary sentence length. One idea per sentence.
- Keep the facts and numbers exactly as in `FACTS.md`.
- End with something concrete (availability, a question, a next step).

## Don't
- No openers like "I hope this finds you well", "I am writing to express my interest", "I'm thrilled/excited/passionate".
- No inflated words: leverage, utilise, spearhead, synergy, cutting-edge, revolutionary, world-class, robust, seamless, delve, tapestry, landscape, journey.
- No empty claims: "quick learner", "team player", "results-driven", "detail-oriented".
- No rule-of-three padding ("fast, scalable and reliable") unless all three are true and needed.
- No em-dash chains or decorative punctuation; no emojis in applications.
- No flattery of the company that could apply to any company ("your innovative work").
- Nothing the candidate couldn't explain in an interview.

## Quick test
1. Could this sentence be sent to another company unchanged? Rewrite it.
2. Would the candidate say this out loud? If not, simplify.
3. Is every number in `FACTS.md`? If not, remove it.
